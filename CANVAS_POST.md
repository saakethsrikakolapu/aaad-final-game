## Provisional Title

**Two Sisters, One Stone**
*Follow the bloodline. Find your way home.*

## Draft Game Instructions

**Overview.** *Two Sisters, One Stone* is a story-driven digital escape room based on Yaa Gyasi's *Homegoing*. Half-sisters Effia and Esi are born to the same mother, Maame, in 18th-century Ghana, but their lives split at Cape Coast Castle: one marries the British governor, and the other is held in the dungeon below and sold into slavery. Your group must follow both bloodlines through four rooms and recover the four pieces of Maame's black stone to escape.

**Players and time.** 3–6 players, one laptop or phone, about 15 minutes.

**Setup.** Open the game link on one device and gather around it. Choose roles: a **Reader** who reads each story intro aloud, a **Driver** who controls the screen, a **Historian** who connects clues to what we studied in class, and a **Skeptic** who double-checks answers before they are submitted.

**How to play.**
1. Click **Begin**. The timer starts.
2. Each room opens with a short story intro. Read it aloud together.
3. Solve the room's puzzle. Puzzles include sorting scenes, rebuilding the family tree, filling in historical blanks, and solving a final riddle. Every answer comes from *Homegoing* and our course material.
4. Click **Check answer**. If you are right, you recover a stone fragment and the next room unlocks. If not, the game tells you how close you are, so talk it through and try again.
5. Stuck? Each room has three hints, each more specific than the last.

**The rooms.** (1) The Castle, (2) The Family Tree, (3) The Mines, (4) Homecoming. Each room moves forward in time, from 18th-century Ghana to the present day.

**Winning.** Recover all four fragments to restore the stone and escape. For an extra challenge, try to finish in under 15 minutes using as few hints as possible.

Progress saves automatically. If the page closes, reopen the link and click **Continue**.

## Sample Element: Room 1, "The Castle"

**Play it:** [game link]

**Story intro.** Maame has two daughters who will never meet. Effia grows up in Fanteland. Esi grows up in Asanteland. Years later, both sisters end up inside the same building: Cape Coast Castle. One lives upstairs, in the governor's quarters. The other is held in the dungeon beneath her feet.

**Puzzle.** Players sort eight "memory" cards into **Upstairs (Effia)** or **The Dungeon (Esi)**:

1. Is the daughter of Big Man, the best warrior in an Asante village.
2. Is married to James Collins, the British governor of Cape Coast Castle.
3. Spends her fifteenth birthday in the women's dungeon, surrounded by hundreds of captive women.
4. Grows up believing that Baaba is her mother.
5. Is handed a black stone, "a piece of your mother," once her marriage to a white man is arranged.
6. Her mother is pushed to choose a house girl, Abronoma, from the war prisoners paraded through their village.
7. Gives birth to a son named Quey.
8. Is forced out of the dungeon before she can dig up her mother's stone.

**Answer key** (page numbers from *Homegoing*):

| Upstairs (Effia) | Dungeon (Esi) |
|---|---|
| 2 (pp. 14, 16) | 1 (pp. 28, 31) |
| 4 (p. 27) | 3 (p. 28) |
| 5 (p. 16) | 6 (p. 35) |
| 7 (pp. 25, 50) | 8 (p. 49) |

The tricky cards are 5 and 8. Both sisters have a black stone from Maame, so players have to remember that Effia receives hers from Baaba, the woman who raised her, and only later learns from Fiifi that Maame was her real mother. Esi received hers from Maame directly and loses it in the dungeon.

**Hints.**
1. Two sisters, one building. One has a home inside the castle. The other is held captive beneath it.
2. Effia grows up in Fanteland and is married off to a British official. Esi grows up in Asanteland and is captured in a raid.
3. Both sisters carry a black stone from Maame, but only one of them knew Maame as her mother growing up.

**After solving: "Why this matters."** Cape Coast Castle is a real place in present-day Ghana. British officials lived and worked on its upper floors while captive Africans were held in the dungeons below, then forced onto ships through what is now called the Door of No Return. Gyasi opens the novel by splitting two sisters across those two floors. The slave trade did not only take people from Africa. It divided families, and it ran on deals between European traders and African leaders, including the Fante village Effia grows up in.
