How to Play

Two half-sisters, Effia and Esi, are born to the same mother, Maame, in 18th-century Ghana. Their families are split apart for seven generations: one line marked by fire, the other by water. Follow both bloodlines through four rooms and recover the four pieces of Maame's black stone.

  1. Gather around one screen. Three to six players. Talk everything through together.
  2. Read the story intro at the start of each room out loud.
  3. Solve the puzzle. Every answer comes from Homegoing and what we studied in class.
  4. Check your answer. A correct answer recovers a stone fragment and unlocks the next room.
  5. Stuck? Each room has three hints, each more specific than the last.
  6. Escape by restoring the whole stone. The timer tracks how long your group takes.

suggested roles

Reader reads intros aloud. Driver controls the screen. Historian connects clues to class. Skeptic double-checks before you submit.

    The Castle, Solved

    Effia walks the halls above the ocean, wearing the stone the woman who raised her pressed into her hand. Below her, Esi waits in the dark, and when the soldiers march her to the ship, her mother's stone stays behind in the dirt.

    Same mother. Same castle. Two completely different futures.

    why this matters

    Cape Coast Castle is a real place in present-day Ghana. British officials lived and worked on its upper floors while captive Africans were held in the dungeons below. From there they were forced onto ships through what is now called the Door of No Return.

    Gyasi opens the novel by splitting two sisters across those two floors. The slave trade did not only take people from Africa. It divided families, and it ran on deals between European traders and African leaders, including the Fante village Effia grows up in.
